About me

I am a PhD researcher at the University of Manchester and a student in the Fusion Power Centre for Doctoral Training (CDT), studying reduced-activation ferritic/martensitic (RAFM) steels for fusion. I hold an MSc in Fusion Energy (Distinction) and a BSc in Physics (First Class) from the University of York.

Alongside my research, I am a Junior Fusion Energy Analyst at Fusion Advisory Services, where I turn technical developments in the fusion sector into clear information for decision-makers. I am also a member of the Institute of Physics.

RAFM Steels for Fusion

Reduced-activation ferritic/martensitic steels are leading candidates for structural components in future fusion power plants. My PhD at the University of Manchester investigates their behaviour under the demands of a fusion environment, as part of the Fusion Power CDT.

Bayesian Optimisation of Tokamak Pedestal Stability

H-mode tokamak plasmas are prone to edge localised modes (ELMs), which the peeling-ballooning model links to the pedestal pressure gradient and toroidal current. I used Bayesian optimisation to choose which points in this parameter space to evaluate, generating equilibria with FreeGS and testing MHD stability with ELITE.

BSc Thesis

Multi-Pulse Laser Wakefield Acceleration

Used particle-in-cell simulations to compare multi-pulse and single-pulse laser wakefield acceleration. Correcting pulse spacing for relativistic electron masses, a five-pulse system achieved self-injection using 0.15 J less energy than a conventional single-pulse approach.
